00 · DIAGNOSIS

Five things showing up everywhere.

Before we get to chart-by-chart redesigns, these themes recur across PibPage, CagedPage, DashboardGeralPage and the rest. Fixing them once at the chart-component level fixes them everywhere.

01 · KPI

KPI cards & the hero strip.

KpiCard currently renders label + value + sub + ⓘ. Three small additions turn it from a labelled number into a fast-readable indicator: an inline delta chip, a sparkline under the value, and a scale unit separate from the value so the typography doesn't collapse.

Current · KpiCard.jsx

PIB Último Ano

DashboardGeralPage → cards[0]
PIB Último Anoi
R$ 1.247.832.450
Ano 2023
  • Value runs to 15 characters; the eye can't compare it to other cards. Use short notation (R$ 1.25 Bi) by default with the long form on hover.
  • No direction — is this up or down vs 2022? The page has the data (resumo.crescimento_percentual) but it's hidden in a separate card.
  • "Ano 2023" steals the "sub" slot. That's a period, not a footnote — belongs in the chip row at the top of the page, not on every card.
  • The ⓘ is the only colored thing, so the eye lands there first.

02 · TIME SERIES

Time series — make the axis disappear, make the story appear.

The AreaLineChart is solid. The biggest wins are axis quieting (one unit caption instead of 5 R$-prefixed labels), benchmark + forecast (already in your backlog), and annotating the events that matter (worst month, new peak, COVID dip) rather than relying on the user to spot them.

Current · AreaLineChart

Evolução Anual do PIB

2010 — 2023
  • "R$" repeated 5 times on the Y axis. Reads as 5 currencies, not 5 numbers.
  • Every point gets a dot + glow halo, including 12 unimportant ones. The eye doesn't know where to land.
  • No benchmark, no peak/trough callouts, no forecast. A prefeito has to mentally compute "is this trajectory good".
  • X-axis stops at the last data point — looks like the chart ended, not the data.

  • Benchmark line at "média estadual" gives every absolute number a relative reading.
  • Forecast tail (dashed, accent-3) extends past the last point — same regression idea already in IDEAS.md.
  • Dots only on moments worth pointing at: first point, COVID trough, current value.
  • Annotation pins replace the "you have to hover to see what happened" model.
03 · STACKED

Stacked composition is two questions, not one.

"Valor Adicionado por Setor" is doing double duty: how big is the total and how big is each setor. A stack on absolute values answers #1 well and #2 badly (you can't compare the middle slices across years). Show both: a stacked-totals chart and a 100% small-multiple share view, toggled.

Current · StackedBarChart

Valor Adicionado por Setor

PIB · 2019 — 2023
Agropecuária Indústria Serviços Governo
  • Middle segments float — try to answer "did Indústria grow from 2019 to 2023?" by eye. You can't.
  • 4 saturated colors all competing for attention. There's no focus.
  • Top-segment radius-5 on every bar is a small but noticeable inconsistency — only the top setor reads as a "real" bar.
Proposed · totals + share toggle

  • Single hue, varying opacity for setors — color stops competing, the legend now describes a scale not a rainbow.
  • Latest year framed in accent-2 + delta in the label — "what changed this year" is the first thing the eye finds.

04 · TWIN / CAGED

CAGED twin bars hide the only number that matters: o saldo.

The current chart asks the eye to subtract the red bar from the green bar, every month. The chart already has all the data — show the saldo directly, with a 0 line, and use the twin bars as context below the headline.

Current · TwinBarChart

Admissões vs. Desligamentos

2024 · mensal
Admissões Desligamentos
  • The actionable number is the saldo: was the month net-positive? The eye can't compute that across 12 months by subtraction.
  • "Saldo" only appears in the hover tooltip — the chart's most important variable is hidden behind interaction.
  • 24 bars at near-equal heights is just visual noise.

  • One zero line is more useful than five money lines.
05 · COMPARISON

Comparing 6 municípios is comparing one + 5 — use focus + context.

The current pattern (one color per município, COMP_COLORS) hits a wall fast. The municipal user has one município they care about. Show theirs in full color, peers in muted grey, with the peer median as a benchmark. Hover restores any city. It scales effortlessly to 20 lines.

Current · MultiLineChart

PIB Comparativo — Municípios

6 cidades · 2010 — 2023
Belo Horizonte Uberlândia Contagem Juiz de Fora Betim Montes Claros
  • 6 saturated lines + 6 swatches force the eye to switch between chart and legend. By the time you've found Uberlândia, you've forgotten what it does.
  • Colors don't carry meaning beyond identity — green doesn't mean "good" here, it just means "Belo Horizonte".
  • Lines tangle near the median where the interesting story usually lives.
Proposed · focus + context

PIB Comparativo — Uberlândia vs. pares

grupo de 6 municípios · mediana destacada
Uberlândia (você) Mediana dos pares 5 municípios pares
  • One color, one job: accent-2 is "you". Everything else is context, intentionally desaturated.
  • Adds a peer median dashed line — the single most useful reference for "are we above or below comparable cities".
  • Hover a peer line → it momentarily takes accent-1 + a label appears. Click → swaps focus to that city.
  • Scales to 10, 20, 50 peer cities without legend chaos.
06 · DONUT

Most donuts want to be a horizontal bar.

Donuts are great for one comparison ("majority vs minority"). They're hard for 4+ categories, hard to label, hard to compare across cards, and effectively impossible to read on mobile. Keep donut for the 2-slice case; use a labelled 100% bar everywhere else.

07 · RANKING

The ranking is the chart — labels belong inside it.

HBarChart renders bars in SVG and floats labels in a separate absolute-positioned DOM layer. It works but it's fragile to widths and font changes. A grid-based row with the bar as background is simpler, more accessible, and lets you highlight "you" without z-index gymnastics.
